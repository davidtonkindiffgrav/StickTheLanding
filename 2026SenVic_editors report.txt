# Editorial Report: "A Weekend to Remember — 2026 Senior Victorian Championships"

**Prepared for:** Stick The Landing
**Date:** 28 May 2026
**Status:** Pre-publication review

---

## Summary

The article is well-written, factually dense, and arithmetically accurate throughout. All scores, margins, and totals in the tables check out precisely. However, there are several issues worth addressing before publication — ranging from a significant factual error about the venue name, to a club name inconsistency, to a structural heading problem and a claim that needs tightening. There are also a few notes about gaps in coverage.

---

## Issues Found

### 1. VENUE NAME — Factual Error (High Priority)

The article states the competition was held at the **"State Netball and Hockey Centre in Parkville."** This is **out of date.** Following a $64.6 million redevelopment completed in 2021, the venue was officially renamed and is now known as **Melbourne Sports Centres – Parkville** (also referred to colloquially as Parkville Stadium). The old name "State Netball Hockey Centre" is listed on Wikipedia and government sources as a *former* name. While the venue is the same building on Brens Drive, using the superseded name is a factual inaccuracy and may confuse readers or undermine the article's credibility.

**Suggested fix:** Replace "State Netball and Hockey Centre in Parkville" with "Melbourne Sports Centres – Parkville" (or "Parkville Stadium" as a shorter alternative).

---

### 2. DUPLICATE H3 HEADING (Structural Issue)

The article opens the WAG section with:

```html
<h3>Women's Artistic Gymnastics</h3>
<p>The Senior Victorian Championships ran across three days...</p>
<h3>The closest finish of the season</h3>
```

The first `<h3>` ("Women's Artistic Gymnastics") is used as a section divider, but then a *second* `<h3>` immediately follows as the first sub-topic. This creates a structural oddity where two `<h3>` elements appear back-to-back with only one paragraph between them. The MAG and Acrobatic sections use the same pattern. This is likely intentional as a design convention, but worth flagging: the sport-category headings ("Women's Artistic Gymnastics," "Men's Artistic Gymnastics," "Acrobatic Gymnastics") are semantically more like `<h2>` section titles, while the sub-topic headings are the true `<h3>` content. If the site's `blog.css` stylesheet treats these differently, the current markup may cause display inconsistencies. Consider either promoting the sport-category headings to `<h2>`, or simply letting them stand if the existing stylesheet renders them correctly.

---

### 3. CLUB NAME INCONSISTENCY — Chamford

In the prose (line 116), Warnes' club is called simply **"Chamford."** In the results table (line 134), it appears as **"Chamford Gymnastics Club."** Neither exactly matches the club's actual registered name, which is **"Chamford Gymnastics"** (no "Club" suffix — confirmed via their website, Instagram, and Facebook page). The table form ("Chamford Gymnastics Club") is incorrect; the prose shortening ("Chamford") is acceptable as informal usage.

**Suggested fix:** Change the table entry from "Chamford Gymnastics Club" to "Chamford Gymnastics." Consider aligning first-reference prose to use the full name on first mention.

---

### 4. GEELONG YMCA CLUB NAME

The article consistently uses **"Geelong YMCA Gym Club"** throughout (tables and prose). Their own materials use both "YMCA Geelong Gymnastics Club" (Facebook page name) and "Geelong YMCA Gym Club" (their own competition voting reference). The abbreviated form appears to be an accepted short form used in competition contexts, so this is not necessarily wrong — but it is worth confirming whether GV results sheets use "Geelong YMCA Gym Club" as the registered competition name, since the Facebook page's official name is "YMCA Geelong Gymnastics Club." If the registered name differs from what the article uses, it should be corrected for consistency.

### 6. PULL QUOTE — Unclear Attribution

The pull quote reads: *"A full point on beam, over four events, that's the championship."* No attribution is given. Pull quotes in sports journalism typically either attribute to a named source (a coach, athlete, official) or are clearly labelled as the writer's own voice/analysis. As written, it reads like a direct quote from someone but has no name attached. Either attribute it (if it came from a real source) or reframe it as authorial commentary rather than a quoted statement.

---

### 7. ACROBATIC GYMNASTICS — TL;DR / Body Inconsistency

The article's TL;DR (summary box) states: *"In Acrobatic Gymnastics, Team Adrenalin led the field across every senior category."* However, the Acrobatic Gymnastics body section says **"at the time of writing, Gymnastics Victoria had not yet published the full results."** These two statements contradict each other. If results were not yet available at time of writing, the TL;DR claim about Team Adrenalin's dominance should either be removed or qualified (e.g., "based on early session reporting"). If the writer has confirmed Team Adrenalin's results from a source other than the GV results sheet (such as the club's own social media), that should be noted.

**Action required:** Either remove/qualify the Team Adrenalin claim from the TL;DR, or add a sentence in the Acrobatic section explaining the source (e.g., club social media post).

---

### 8. "TEN WEEKS" CLAIM — Minor Imprecision

The article states Warnes' season gain of 2.683 points came "over ten weeks." The events cited are:
- Knox Senior WAG Invitational (March)
- BTYC MAG and WAG Senior Extravaganza
- State Trial 1
- Victorian Championships (22–24 May)

If the Knox Invitational was in early March and the championships ended 24 May, that is approximately 11–12 weeks, not 10. This is a minor point but worth verifying against the actual competition dates to ensure accuracy.

---

### 9. EKGA SECTION — First Names Only (Style Note)

The EKGA selections paragraph refers to the club's first-ever WAG athlete as **"Mila"** and judge as **"Louisa"** — first names only, no surnames. This is inconsistent with the rest of the article, which always uses full names (first + last) for athletes. If full surnames are known and can be confirmed, they should be added. If the club's own announcement used first names only, a note explaining this ("whose full name was not provided") or simply omitting the names in favour of "one WAG athlete" would be cleaner.

---

### 10. META DESCRIPTION vs. BODY — Minor Discrepancy

The HTML `<meta name="description">` says Cerano Tosone "swept five of six Level 7 apparatus events" — which matches the body text. However, the body elaborates that parallel bars was **shared** with Meos on equal scores. This is correctly handled in the body but the meta description and OG description could more precisely say "won or shared five of six" if SEO accuracy is a concern.
