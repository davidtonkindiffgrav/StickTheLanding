Who's Going to States? Our 2026 Regional Championship Predictions

Crunching every 2026 WAG result to guess who clears the qualifying scores at this weekend's seven Regional Championships, region by region

David "Tonko" Tonkin  ·  2 September 2026

We are almost at the end of the competitive season for WAG and MAG gymnastics in Victoria, and I thought it would be fun to dive into the data and make some predictions about what we could expect at each of the regional championship events that will be taking place this coming weekend.

But firstly, I want to preface this piece with some important context. I am a data nerd and I like graphs and charts, but first and foremost, I am a Dad whose little girl does gymnastics. So as much as it's fun to look at the data, I want to be upfront and remind us all (including myself) that 90% of the gymnasts I write about are just kids who are working their butts off in a sport they love. A sport that can be cruel, rewarding, painful, frustrating and just plain hard! Unlike traditional athletics where it's as simple as run the fastest and you win, or jump the highest and you win, this is a sport where you can put in hundreds of hours of hard work, only to have a 50 year old man who hasn't touched his toes in 10 years take a point off because, perish the thought, you dare wobble a tiny bit while you're more than a metre off the ground on a tiny piece of wood after doing a triple wolf turn. So before we take these numbers too seriously, let's not forget that for every 1st, 2nd and 3rd there is also probably a 20th, 30th or even 40th place. And the kids in those places don't love the sport any less than the ones on the podium. Arguably they love it even more! Because despite results, they keep coming back to gym and competing each year. So if you are someone who has had a rough season, or you don't think you will qualify for states, I want to applaud you for all the hard work, the conditioning, the fighting through the pain of that injury, or skipping that birthday party to go to training. And since this site is all about the data, I want to give you some numbers that might make you feel better about not placing on the podium. There are 28 million people in Australia and approximately 230,000 registered gymnasts. That means less than a single percent of people can do what you do. So 1st or 50th, remember that you are part of the 1% and you are amazing.

So with that said, let's break down what the numbers say, region by region, and take some guesses about who we might see at States later this year!

A note on scope: this article covers WAG only. There weren't as many MAG competitions this season, or at least the results weren't posted as often, so between that and my limited time, I'm just doing WAG for this one.

How we're guessing this

Gymnastics Victoria's qualification rules run to pages of handbook language, and I'll be upfront: what follows is my own reading of it, not an official interpretation, so take it as a gym dad's best attempt at parsing a long document rather than gospel.

Levels 3 and 4 work differently to Levels 5-7. At Levels 3-4, there's no qualifying score to hit at all, it's a straight numbers game. Each level and division gets a fixed number of state qualifying spots, split up across Victoria's seven regions based on how many teams that region fields. We worked out our own version of that region-by-region slot table from our own results data (it isn't an official published document, so there's a chance our numbers aren't 100% precise), and that's what I mean whenever I say "slots" below.

Levels 5-7 add a qualifying score into the mix. Reading through the handbook, the process looks roughly like this: first, the single best team in each region gets a spot, but only if it clears a minimum team score. Then each region gets up to four more spots for its best individual all-arounders, provided they clear an individual qualifying score too. After that, there's a further pool of wildcard spots handed out by score regardless of region. The wildcard mechanics in particular are genuinely fuzzy to me from the handbook alone, so we've left those out of the tables below and stuck to the two things we could actually model with confidence: the single team spot, and each region's top individual qualifiers.

One more wrinkle: Division 1 at Levels 5-7 is split into Under and Open age brackets with slightly different individual qualifying scores (the team score is the same either way). Our results data doesn't reliably separate the two for most competitions, so we've used the more lenient Under threshold across the board for Division 1. If you're chasing the Open score specifically, treat our Division 1 individual cutoffs as a floor, not the real number.

For "who's in form," I went with two numbers: each team or gymnast's best score from a 2026 competition, and their median score across however many times they've competed this season. Best score is what we used for the actual predictions below, since it's the same "did you clear the number" logic Gymnastics Victoria itself will apply on the day. That does mean our predictions are effectively assuming everyone hits their season-best on the day, which is optimistic by definition, so treat them as "who's capable of qualifying if it all comes together" rather than "who will." Where the median sits well below the best, we've flagged it as a Large Deviation, worth an eye if you like a good upset, since it's a sign the best score might have been more of a one-off than the norm.

One more thing worth flagging: plenty of clubs run more than one team in the same level and division, especially at Levels 3 and 4. Gymnastics Victoria's slot allocation is genuinely per-team, not per-club, so a club with two strong squads can occupy two of a region's slots. Where we could tell two of a club's squads apart, usually by colour, Red, Blue, Navy and so on, sometimes a club actually confirmed which was which for us, and occasionally there's no colour at all and we've just called them Team 1 and Team 2, we've kept them as separate rows in the tables below rather than merging them into one score for the club. That's also why you'll sometimes see a club listed once with no colour at all: either it only fielded one team all season, or we simply couldn't tell its squads apart from the data we have, so we've left it as a single combined line rather than guess.

A couple of honest data gaps before we get into it. Jets Gymnastics runs squads out of six different venues (Diamond Creek, Eltham, Grovedale, Shepparton, Bendigo and Gisborne) spread across different regions. We can usually place a Jets team result because the team sheets often name the venue, but the individual all-around results almost never do, competition organisers typically just print "JET" for every Jets gymnast regardless of which club they train at, so we've got no reliable way to sort them by venue ourselves. That means Jets gymnasts are under-represented in the individual columns below even where their teams are properly placed. And the North East and South East regions simply have fewer results in our database than the rest of the state, both regions run fewer big invitationals and results don't always make it online as quickly, so treat those two sections as more of a "who we know about" list than a complete picture.

Whatever the numbers say

I want to end where I started. Everything above is a guess, built from season-best scores and a layperson's reading of a qualification handbook, and Gymnastics Victoria's actual regional results this weekend are the only numbers that count. Teams have lineup changes. Kids have good days and bad days. A gymnast who's never troubled the individual qualifying score before can produce the routine of their season when it matters most, and one of our projected favourites can just as easily have an off day. That's sport, and it's a big part of why we watch.

So to every gymnast heading into a Regional Championship this weekend, whether your name is in one of the tables above or not: go get it. And if the scoreboard doesn't go your way, remember what I said at the top. You're already in the 1%. Good luck out there.
