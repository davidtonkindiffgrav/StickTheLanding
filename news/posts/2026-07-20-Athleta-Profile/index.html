Athleta was a bit of a mystery to me until I started pulling data for this site. It's out in Altona North, on the western side of town, a fair way from the eastern and southern venues where my daughter's circuit usually runs, and in years of turning up to comps I don't think our paths had ever crossed. That's more a comment on geography than on the club: the data says they've been quietly building something solid.

Last time, I profiled Knox Gymnastics Club, a club I've been to a few times over the years, including this past weekend for their Junior Invitational (keep an eye out for that results report once the full scores are released). Athleta is close to the opposite: a club I'd genuinely never come across before I started this site.

The comeback

Athleta Gymnastics opened its doors in Altona North on 23 January 2018. It didn't take long to make a mark: the club's own history page credits its first year with being crowned Best New Club of the Year, alongside a 3rd-place Level 5 team finish at the Victorian Championships. Then, like every other Victorian club, COVID shut training down through 2020 and into 2021.

Athleta reopened in 2022 "with renewed energy and vision," by its own description, and the comeback had some genuine milestones attached: a feature in the Herald Sun in April, a 2nd-place team finish at the Australian Gymnastics Championships in May, and a trip to the National Clubs Carnival in October. The following year, the club's Level 5 WAG team was crowned Victorian Champions, and five athletes made the trip to the Australian Championships. This year, Athleta picked up nominations across four categories at the Gymnastics Victoria Awards Night, including Small Club of the Year.

What the numbers say: WAG

Thirty-six WAG athletes appear in my database for Athleta, with 177 results across 22 competitions spanning the 2024, 2025, and 2026 seasons. At Level 8 and above, the club has accumulated 22 medals in my data: 10 gold, 4 silver, and 8 bronze. Floor is where the numbers show the club's biggest relative strength against the field.

Chontelle Burgess had about as good a 2024 as shows up anywhere in this database: four starts at international level, four wins, capped by the WAG Senior Victorian Championships with 52.05. She's kept winning since, taking the BTYC WAG Extravaganza and the Senior Judges Invitational in 2025, then opening 2026 with wins at the Senior Judges Invitational and at WAG Trial 1 / Border Challenge Trial 1. Athleta's own history page notes that she made the Junior International Team Vic, the state squad, in 2025, which won gold at the Australian Championships, a result that sits above what my Victoria-only database can show.

Darby Weston has climbed alongside her. She was a Level 8 athlete in 2024, finishing 3rd twice, then moved up to international level in 2025 and won the BTYC WAG Extravaganza. This year she won the Waverley Invite outright. Per the club, she's also the reigning Australian champion in the Future International category, again a result my database can't independently confirm. Athleta's history page separately notes a Border Challenge floor medal for the programme in July 2025, with Team Vic taking out the overall title; the club doesn't name the athlete involved, and I can't match it to a specific result in my data, but it's worth a mention regardless.

Further down the club, Amelie Tinkler is the clearest junior form line. She finished mid-pack through 2024 at Level 7, 14th, 22nd, and 11th across three starts, then turned it around in 2025 with wins at the BTYC Junior Invite and the Metro West Regional Championships, plus a 2nd at the Niddrie Invitational.

What the numbers say: MAG

Athleta's men's programme is small, just five athletes in my database, with 54 results across 19 competitions since 2024, so there's not a lot to read into yet. Vault is the strongest apparatus relative to the field. Ryan Voinescu has been the club's standard-bearer at Level 8 since 2024, and this year placed 2nd at the Senior Victorian Championships with 65.85. Behind him, Cahyo Dunningham and Dominic Ficarra have moved up from Level 5 to Level 6 together over the past two seasons, and Henry Mathieson's first results in my database come at Level 5 this year.

Worth checking in on

A club two years removed from a full shutdown, with an unbeaten season on the books for its top senior and a men's programme just getting off the ground, is one worth another look once the men's squad has a bit more data behind it.
